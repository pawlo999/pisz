# Pisz — plan

A writing app for a four-year-old: capital letters, digits and the
pre-writing strokes, in Polish and Norwegian, on an iPad with a finger.
Litery's sibling: Litery teaches her to *recognise* letters, Pisz to
*form* them. Same rules where they overlap — web app, no asset files,
the device's own voices, her name never in the source, mastery rebuilt
from a log so two devices merge.

## 1 · What the research says, and what the app does with it

| Finding | Source | In the app |
|---|---|---|
| At 4 a child copies │ ─ ○ (age 3) and + (4y); / at 4y4m, □ 4y6m, \ 4y7m, ✕ 4y11m, △ 5y3m. Full reports with every citation: four research passes on 6 Oct 2026

**The premise worth arguing with:** "learn a lot each time". Every line
above points the other way — a little, every day, spread out. Litery's
own data said the same: thirteen letters at once dropped her to 54% and
35% first-try on 23 Sep.

## 2 · One letter's journey

    level 0  new         → demo, road, dots, copy   (one item, ~1 minute)
    level 1  traced road → dots, copy
    level 2  traced dots → copy
    level 3  copied      → from memory, next visit
    level 4  from memory → from memory, spaced out
    level 5  owned       — from memory on two different days

Two misses in a row from memory or when copying step it back one level.
Starting in the wrong place or going the wrong way twice in the last
three puts one trace over the dots in front of the next item — the only
step that physically will not let her do it backwards. Mirrored letters
never count as misses.

Shapes stop at copying; they never need memory work.

## 3 · A sitting

1. a warm-up shape — the next one to learn, or the stalest
2. letters in progress, least known first
3. at most one new letter, her name's letters first
4. reviews that are due
5. her name, once every letter in it can be copied
6. topped up with owned things she has not seen for longest

Shown as a row of bubbles; the suggested one breathes, any undone one can
be tapped. The first ever sitting is │ ─ ○ and nothing else: success on
day one.

## 4 · How her writing is judged

**Tracing** follows one template stroke at a time and only moves forward
along it, so a stroke begun at the far end, or drawn backwards, never
completes — no separate direction rule needed. A lifted finger keeps its
progress; lifting within 10% of the end finishes the stroke.

**Free writing** fits her ink onto the template (scale, some stretch,
shift), then asks: is every stroke there (coverage), and is everything
she drew part of the letter (precision)? Points only match when they are
close *and* their lines run the same way. Then:

- a mark (accent, dot, ring, slash) must be its own short stroke, so a
  missing one is named: "A kreska?" and she can still add it;
- for letters that are not their own mirror the mirrored template is
  always tried — if it fits clearly better she wrote it backwards;
- the six closest other glyphs are tried too — if one fits clearly better,
  it was another letter.

Measured on synthetic child handwriting (wobble, tilt, wrong size, strokes
that miss each other), gentle setting, all 60 glyphs:

| | |
|---|---|
| her attempt accepted — moderate wobble | 99.7% |
| her attempt accepted — heavy wobble | 96.7% |
| another letter accepted | 1.3% |
| scribble accepted | 3.3% |
| forgotten mark caught and named | 90.9% |
| mirrored letter recognised | 81% |
| time per verdict (node) | 28 ms |

Synthetic is not her. **The tolerances are a first guess until her own
drawings come in** — every copy and memory attempt is stored, accepted or
not, so they can be re-judged against new thresholds offline.

## 5 · What the parent sees

On the iPad (long-press the gear), and on any phone at `parent.html` — **one
dashboard for both apps** (his call, 7 Oct): the same sync key opens Litery's
record and Pisz's, with "five minutes together today" on top (the letters she
is on now, and to say the sound, not the name):

- days played, minutes, sittings, % right
- every shape and letter as a colour grid by level
- for what she has started: tries, % right, last seen, and what keeps
  happening (wrong start, wrong way, mirrored, forgot the accent)
- **her handwriting over time** — up to eight of her own attempts per
  letter, oldest to newest, the not-yet-accepted ones outlined
- the next sitting, item by item
- the last device check, and any app errors (with the build)

Settings: letters per sitting, sittings per day, strictness, numbers on or
off, left hand, speech speed, drawing sound, language, test run, sync key,
copy/merge data, change name.

## 6 · Rulings

- **Web app, iPad first.** Same reasons as Litery; nothing here needs
  native. If iPadOS fails it, the same files run on Android.
- **Capitals only for now.** Norwegian and Polish schools move to
  lowercase and cursive at 6–7; at 4 the capitals' motor simplicity and
  Litery's capitals win. Lowercase is a later stage.
- **Finger, not stylus.** The one trial says so. A pen is supported.
- **Rewards are her own work**, never a currency.
- **Separate app, shared sync.** Its own home-screen icon and its own
  code, so Litery's working game cannot be broken by it; its own worker
  reading and writing only `pisz:` keys in Litery's KV namespace, so one
  sync key can serve both apps.
- **Never `localStorage.clear()`, never a key without `pisz.`** — in Safari
  the two apps share the origin.

## 7b · Words and names (his calls, 6–7 Oct)

- Shapes are called what they are: kreska w dół, kreska w bok, kółko, skośna
  kreska w lewo / w prawo, kwadrat, zygzak (NO: strek ned, strek bortover, ring,
  skrå strek mot venstre / høyre, firkant, sikksakk). A picture only where it looks
  like the shape: góra ⛰️, tęcza 🌈, fale 🌊.
- One rule for Polish letters: the letter starts its word → "s jak sowa";
  otherwise "ą jak w słowie wąż" (never a declined form: "w wężu" loses the ą).
- Picture words start with one plain sound: sowa, gęś, pomidor (not słoń,
  gwiazda, pies).
- Letter sounds are recorded by a parent, once, on `record.html` (his call
  7 Oct, "1a"): the iPad voice can only say a letter's name ("gie"), it fails
  at a held sound ("uuu"), and the free recordings were five men's voices next
  to a woman's TTS, with b d g k p t only as consonant+vowel. A recorded letter
  is heard as the recording, then the voice's "jak gęś". Both apps play them
  (Litery has its own copy of `sounds.js`), stored on the sync service under
  the family key (`/a/<key>/<lang>/<letter>`, WAV, 22 kHz mono), cached on the
  device for offline. Shared sounds: PL ó = u; NB c = k (its word is cowboy),
  z = s, w = v. No recording, not loaded in 0.7 s, or audio not running: the
  old phrase from the voice — never "jak gęś" alone.
- He recorded all 31 Polish sounds on 7 Oct and will not record Norwegian
  (not his language). So a Norwegian consonant that sounds as the Polish one
  does plays the Polish recording until a Norwegian one exists (b4, his "1a"):
  b d f g j k l m n p s t, and v from Polish w. Not the vowels — a Norwegian
  vowel's name is its sound, so the voice already says it — and not h (Polish
  /x/) or r (his is rolled). record.html marks those rows "now uses your
  Polish …"; a Norwegian recording replaces it.

- **She watches first** while the guide shows a letter on her pad (a step's
  first showing, the replay after a miss, 👀): the pad takes ink at "Teraz

  the 7 Oct "draw during the guide" — watching the movement first is what the

- **The right way shown, never rejected (1a):** a copy or memory letter right
  in shape but begun in the wrong place or drawn the wrong way counts, and she

  line is drawn again — on the figure when it is shown, on her pad in memory.
  Before b7, 11 of her 29 accepted letters had a wrong start and heard plain
  praise. Berninger 1997: numbered arrows + memory; Cimpian 2007 / Gunderson

- **↩️ (2a)** next to 🧽 takes back only her last line — in dots (with its
